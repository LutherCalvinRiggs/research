## TL;DR
Credit card optimization ("churning") is the practice of systematically acquiring cards with new-account bonuses, using each bonus, and cycling through new cards over time. The decision framework has five sequential questions: (1) Are you below the application velocity threshold? (2) Which point ecosystem serves your travel goals? (3) Do you have business income that qualifies for business cards? (4) What is your minimum spend capacity? (5) Are you optimizing for cash back or travel points? Each question gates the next. Violating velocity thresholds or applying in the wrong sequence forfeits the bonuses or triggers application denials.

## The Five Sequential Questions

### Question 1: Application Velocity — Are You Below the Threshold?

Every major issuer applies implicit or explicit limits on how many cards you can hold or how recently you applied. These rules determine eligibility for new-account bonuses.

**The 5/24 concept (generalized):** Some issuers automatically deny applicants who have opened more than a threshold number of personal credit cards in the prior 24 months — regardless of credit score or income. This threshold is the primary gating rule for new churners. If you're above it, applications for this issuer's cards will be denied.

**Why it matters:** New-account bonuses (sign-up bonuses / SUBs) are the primary value source in churning. An application that gets denied wastes a hard inquiry and produces no bonus. Velocity management — knowing when you're eligible — is the foundational skill.

**Application spacing:** Beyond per-issuer rules, applying too frequently across all issuers signals risk to credit bureaus. Experienced churners typically space personal card applications 2-3 months apart minimum, and track every application date.

**The 24-month clock:** Most new-account bonuses have a "once per 24 months" or "once per lifetime" restriction — you must not have received the bonus on that same card (or family of cards) within a defined lookback window. Check this before applying.

---

### Question 2: Point Ecosystem — Which Travel Program?

If your goal is travel (not cash back), you must choose a point ecosystem before selecting cards. Points from different ecosystems don't transfer between each other and have very different redemption values.

**The three primary ecosystems:**

**Transferable points programs** — Points transfer to multiple airline/hotel partners. Highest ceiling value but require research to maximize. Best for flexible travelers who can shift travel dates.

**Airline miles programs** — Points tied to one airline (or alliance). Best redemption value on that airline's premium cabin flights. Poor value for cash redemption. Best for travelers loyal to one carrier or alliance.

**Hotel points programs** — Points tied to one hotel chain. Best value for free nights at high-category properties. Typically poor airline redemption.

**The ecosystem lock-in problem:** Once you accumulate large point balances in one ecosystem, you're committed to using them there. Choose an ecosystem aligned with where you actually want to go and how you want to fly/stay — not just which sign-up bonus is currently highest.

**Ecosystem selection criteria:**
- Which airline hubs are nearest to you?
- Do you have status with any carrier already?
- Do you prefer flexibility (transferable) or simplicity (cash back)?
- What are your top 2-3 travel destinations?

### Question 3: Business Cards — Do You Qualify?

Business cards operate under separate velocity rules from personal cards in most issuer systems. This is critical because:

1. Business cards typically don't count against the 5/24-equivalent velocity threshold (for most issuers)
2. Business cards often have higher sign-up bonuses and higher minimum spend requirements
3. Business cards have a separate application queue — you can be at the velocity limit for personal cards while still eligible for business cards

**Who qualifies:** You don't need a registered LLC or S-Corp. Sole proprietorships count. Freelance income, eBay/Etsy selling, consulting, driving for rideshare, rental income — all are legitimate small business income. You apply as a sole proprietor using your SSN.

**The sequencing implication:** Experienced churners often pursue business cards first or interleave them with personal cards specifically to avoid consuming personal card slots.

**Risk:** Business cards may still be denied if the issuer deems your business income insufficient relative to the requested credit limit. Some issuers have their own business card velocity rules.

---

### Question 4: Minimum Spend Capacity

Every new-account bonus requires spending a threshold amount within a defined window (typically 3-6 months). This is the minimum spend requirement (MSR).

**The capacity question:** How much organic spending can you route through a new card in 3 months without manufactured spend?

**Calculating your capacity:**
- Monthly recurring: rent/mortgage (if landlord accepts credit), utilities, subscriptions, insurance
- Variable: groceries, gas, dining, travel
- Upcoming large purchases: home improvement, medical, tax payments

**The sequencing implication:** Apply for higher-MSR cards when you have upcoming large purchases. Apply for lower-MSR cards during normal spending months. Attempting to meet an MSR you can't organically hit requires manufactured spend (gift card reselling, etc.) — a separate skillset with its own risks.

**Multiple cards simultaneously:** Experienced churners sometimes open 2-3 cards in a batch and spread spending across all of them, meeting all MSRs simultaneously. This requires careful tracking to avoid missing a deadline.

---

### Question 5: Cash Back vs. Travel Points

If you've answered Questions 1-4 and determined eligibility, ecosystem, and spend capacity — the final decision is whether to optimize for travel or cash.

**Cash back:** Simple, liquid, universally useful. No partner transfer complexity. No blackout dates. No category restrictions on redemption. The value ceiling is lower (typically 1.5-2% effective return vs. 5-10%+ on premium travel via points), but the floor is more predictable.

**Travel points:** Higher ceiling value but requires work to extract maximum value. Premium cabin redemptions (business/first class) are where points beat cash decisively — often by 5-10×. Economy redemptions are often mediocre vs. cash. Hotel points shine at high-category properties.

**The rule of thumb:** If you don't fly internationally in premium cabins at least once a year, cash back may produce more actual value despite the lower nominal rate. Points are most powerful for the specific use case they were designed for.

## Application Strategy Principles

### The 2-Player Strategy
Two-player households can double bonus accumulation by having each person apply independently. Rules apply per person, not per household. Coordination prevents both players from being above the velocity threshold simultaneously.

### The Velocity Clock Management
Track every application date. Know your current count against every issuer's threshold. Plan 12 and 24 months ahead — when existing cards drop off your count, which new cards become available?

### Downgrade vs. Cancel
Cards with annual fees shouldn't automatically be cancelled when the fee posts. Options:
- **Product change (downgrade)** — convert to a no-annual-fee version of the same card. Preserves credit history and credit limit. Keeps the account open. Usually allowed after 12 months.
- **Cancel** — removes the annual fee but may affect credit utilization and average account age.
- **Retention offer** — call the issuer before paying the annual fee. Issuers sometimes offer statement credits, bonus points, or waived fees to retain accounts. Always call.

### The Minimum Spend Timeline
Set a calendar reminder the day you receive a new card: when does the MSR window close? Missing the deadline forfeits the entire bonus regardless of how much you spent. Track this obsessively.

### Authorized Users
Adding authorized users to your card may count against their velocity threshold with some issuers. Don't add AUs carelessly if they're actively pursuing bonuses. Conversely, being added as an AU on someone else's card may help or hurt your own eligibility depending on the issuer.

## The Limitations (Per the Original Flowchart)

**This framework is a general guide, not absolute truth.** Specific rules change. Data points vary. What's true today may not be true in 6 months.

**Individual circumstances matter:** Credit score, income, existing relationship with the issuer, and geographic factors all affect approval odds. The framework assumes reasonably good credit (700+) as a baseline.

**Not a replacement for current research:** Before any application, verify current bonus amounts, current terms, and current data points on approval rates. The r/churning wiki and weekly threads are the living sources; this note is the decision framework.

**Manufactured spend is out of scope:** This framework covers organic spending strategies. Manufactured spend (gift card liquidation, money orders, etc.) is a separate discipline with its own risk profile and is not covered here.

## Questions & Gaps
- The 5/24-equivalent rules vary significantly by issuer — some count only personal cards, some count business cards too, some use different lookback windows. The genericized version necessarily flattens this complexity.
- The "once per 24 months / lifetime" bonus restriction varies: some issuers use card-level restrictions, some use product-family restrictions, some are lifetime. Verifying the specific restriction before applying is always necessary.
- Point valuations change as programs devalue. A 2025 redemption value for a given program may be materially different in 2026. Always verify current redemption rates before making decisions based on projected point values.
